# Threats to Validity and Limitations — Manuscript-Ready v0.1

The current evaluation has several limitations that directly bound the interpretation of SemRisk. First, the operational Jira-style schema and the DS-003 pharmaceutical evidence materially influenced conceptualization and mapping. They therefore provide valid design, reconciliation, mapping, and application evidence, but they cannot independently validate the same semantic commitments. To control this circularity, SemRisk assigns explicit evidence roles and preserves DS-002 as a potential record-level holdout; however, independent transferability testing has not yet been executed.

Second, the pharmaceutical case is intentionally bounded. DS-003 is a purposive qualitative source and supports source-grounded themes and a constructed semantic case, not prevalence estimates, population-level causal claims, treatment effectiveness, or general Pharma validity. The executable end-to-end scenario additionally contains explicitly synthetic trigger, event, control, workflow, responsibility, and reassessment artifacts. These elements test representation and application behavior only. DS-004 remains unavailable for evaluated file-level robustness because its exact files, version, license, checksums, and schema have not yet been frozen.

Third, current semantic validation is incomplete. Formal verification, foundational analysis, competency-question regression, negative controls, and application tests are available, but the predeclared expert semantic-validation protocol has not yet received real reviewer responses. Formal consistency, satisfiability, successful SHACL checks, or executable queries must therefore not be interpreted as expert or domain validation.

Fourth, relational fidelity is task-specific. In the predeclared SQL↔SPARQL comparison, five tasks were equivalent for the tested task, two required declared normalization, and one remained partial because the relational projection records evidence support role more explicitly than the RDF fixture. Moreover, relational constraints operate under application-level closed-world assumptions that differ from OWL's open-world semantics. The results therefore support selected projection tasks rather than a global or lossless ontology–database equivalence claim.

Fifth, comparative and standards evidence remains bounded. Missing or inaccessible comparator/standard documentation is retained as UNKNOWN or an evidence limitation rather than counted as a SemRisk advantage. Current standards results support terminology/process alignment statements but do not establish conformance or certification. The closest-work saturation and some clause/schema-level evidence remain under active completion.

Finally, reproducibility is constrained by data and licensing boundaries. The semantic candidate, relational projection, synthetic/constructed fixtures, and automated evaluation pipeline can be rebuilt from version-controlled artifacts and pinned tools. The private operational spreadsheet cannot be publicly redistributed, and some external standards or datasets may remain license/access restricted. Reproducibility claims therefore apply to the release-bound public/synthetic package and to metadata/hash-based traceability for restricted evidence, not to unrestricted reproduction of every source byte.

These limitations are treated as claim constraints rather than post-hoc caveats. Final manuscript wording is calibrated against them before assurance and release.
